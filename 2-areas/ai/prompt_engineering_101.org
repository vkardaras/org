#+title: Prompt Engineering 101

* Detailing for clarity

** What is prompt engineering?

Prompt engineering involves crafting the perfect "recipe" or prompt to get the best possible output from an AI system.

It's called "engineering" because it involves a systematic approach to designing prompts. You mix and match different elements, such as context, instructions, and examples, to guide the AI towards generating the desired response.

** Why Detail Matters

Imagine you're asking a friend for advice on healthy eating. If you simply say, "Tell me about healthy eating," you might get a general answer that doesn't really help you. But if you ask, "What are the best plant-based proteins for building muscle?" you'll likely get a more specific and useful response.

The same applies to AI! The level of detail in your query determines the quality of the AI's answer.

** The Building Blocks of a Precise Query

To create a precise query, make sure to include:

1. Specificity: Be clear about what you want to know.
2. Context: Give some background information to help the AI understand your situation.
3. Objective: Explain what you want to achieve with the information.

** Crafting Your Query: Step by Step

Let's break it down into easy steps:

*** Step 1: Know What You Want 🤔
Be specific about what you need to know. Instead of just saying "learn to code," think about what exactly you want to learn, like "I want to learn Python for data analysis."

*** Step 2: Add Some Context 🌍
Give the AI some background information to help it understand your query better. For example, "I have a background in statistics and want to learn Python for data analysis."

*** Step 3: Be Clear About Your Goal 🎯
Explain what you hope to achieve with the information. For instance, "I'm looking for a 3-month learning plan to master Python for data analysis, suitable for someone with a statistics background."

** Examples: From Generic to Precise

Let's look at a few examples of how to turn a generic query into a precise one.

*** Example 1: Climate Change 🌍
- *Generic*: "Tell me about climate change."
- *Precise*: "How is climate change affecting the frequency of hurricanes in the Atlantic over the past decade?"
*** Example 2: Healthy Eating 🥗
- *Generic*: "How to eat healthy?"
- *Precise*: "What are the dietary recommendations for maintaining low blood sugar levels, especially for diabetics?"
*** Example 3: Learning to Code 💻
- *Generic*: "How do I learn to code?"
- *Precise*: "What resources and strategies are most effective for a beginner to learn JavaScript for web development, with a focus on interactive websites?"

* Delimiters

** What are Delimiters?

Think of delimiters as the traffic signs 🚦 of your AI prompts. Just like how traffic signs tell drivers when to stop, go, or turn, delimiters tell the AI where different parts of your prompt begin and end. They're like special characters or sequences that mark the boundaries of different sections in your text.

** Why Use Delimiters?

1. 🎯 Enhanced Clarity: Delimiters make your prompts more readable, both for you and the AI. It's like organizing your closet – everything is easier to find when it's neatly separated!
2. 🗂️ Contextual Separation: They help in separating different pieces of information, allowing the AI to apply specific instructions to each part. It's like having different folders for different subjects in your file cabinet.
3. 🎯 Improved Accuracy: By providing a clear structure, delimiters help the AI generate more accurate and relevant responses. It's like giving the AI a well-defined road map to follow!

** Examples of Effective Delimiter Use

Let's explore some examples of how delimiters can be used effectively in prompt engineering.

*** Example 1: Triple Backticks for Code Blocks

Imagine you want the AI to analyze a piece of code. You can use triple backticks ``` to enclose the code block, like this:

Please analyze the following Python code:
#+begin_src python
  def greet(name):
    print(f"Hello, {name}!")
  
  greet("Alice")
#+end_src

The AI will now know exactly which part of your prompt is the code it needs to focus on. It's like highlighting the important parts in your textbook! 📚

*** Example 2: Triple Quotes for Long Text
If you have a long piece of text that you want the AI to process, you can use triple quotes """ to mark the beginning and end of the text, like this:

#+begin_example
Please summarize the main points of the following article:

"""
In recent years, the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) has transformed various industries, from healthcare and finance to transportation and entertainment. AI technologies, such as machine learning and natural language processing, have enabled computers to perform tasks that previously required human intelligence, such as recognizing speech, identifying objects in images, and making predictions based on data patterns.

The potential benefits of AI are vast, ranging from improved efficiency and accuracy in business processes to personalized recommendations and enhanced user experiences. However, the development and deployment of AI also raise important ethical and societal questions, such as the impact on jobs, privacy concerns, and the need for responsible and transparent AI systems.

As AI continues to evolve and become more sophisticated, it is crucial for individuals, organizations, and policymakers to engage in ongoing discussions and collaborations to ensure that the benefits of AI are realized while addressing the challenges and risks associated with this transformative technology.
"""
#+end_example

The AI will now treat the enclosed text as a single unit and provide a concise summary. It's like telling your friend, "Listen carefully, I have something important to tell you!" 🗣️

*** Example 3: XML-style Tags for Structured Data
If you have structured data, like a list of items with their properties, you can use XML-style tags to organize the information, like this:

#+begin_example
Please provide a brief description of the following fruits:

<fruit>
<name>Apple</name>
<color>Red, Green, Yellow</color>
<taste>Sweet, slightly tart</taste>
</fruit>

<fruit>
<name>Banana</name>
<color>Yellow</color>
<taste>Sweet, creamy</taste>
</fruit>
#+end_example

The AI will now understand that you have two separate fruits with their own properties. It's like putting your toys in different boxes based on their type! 🧸📦

*** Example 4: Keyword Delimiters for Structured Prompts
Keyword delimiters are like having a template 📑 for your prompts. They tell the AI what each part of your prompt is about. Just use a specific keyword followed by a colon, and the AI will know exactly what to do with that section! It's like filling out a form with clear labels. 📝

#+begin_example
Analyze the following customer review:   
Review: I recently purchased this smartphone and I'm thoroughly impressed with its features. The camera quality is exceptional, and the battery life is outstanding. The only downside is that the phone is a bit heavy, but it's a small price to pay for such a great device.   
Summary: Please provide a brief summary of the customer's experience with the smartphone.   
Sentiment: Determine the overall sentiment of the review (positive, negative, or neutral).   
Aspects: List the specific aspects of the smartphone mentioned in the review.
#+end_example

In this example, the keyword delimiters are Review, Summary, Sentiment, and Aspects. The AI will now understand that you want a summary of the review, the overall sentiment, and the specific aspects mentioned. It's like giving the AI a template to fill out! 📋

*** Example 5: Multiple Keyword Delimiters for Complex Prompts
Sometimes, you might have a prompt that's more complex than a simple question. That's where multiple keyword delimiters come in handy! 🛠️ In this example, we use the following delimiters: Product, Features, Target Audience, Task, and Requirements. Each delimiter helps provide specific information or instructions to the AI. It's like giving the AI a detailed project brief to work with! 📝

#+begin_example
Product: Acme Smartwatch 
Features: 
- 1.5-inch OLED touchscreen display 
- Heart rate and sleep monitoring 
- GPS tracking and navigation 
- Water-resistant up to 50 meters 
- 7-day battery life   
Target Audience: The Acme Smartwatch is designed for fitness enthusiasts and individuals who lead an active lifestyle.   
Task: Create a product description for the Acme Smartwatch.   
Requirements: 
- Highlight the key features and benefits of the smartwatch
- Explain how the smartwatch caters to the needs of the target audience 
- Use persuasive language to encourage potential customers to purchase the product 
- Keep the description concise, between 150-200 words
#+end_example

With all this information neatly organized using keyword delimiters, the AI will know exactly what the product is, who it's for, and what kind of description you need. It's like giving the AI a super clear set of instructions to follow! 🎯

** Conclusion

Delimiters are like the secret sauce 🥫 in your prompt engineering toolkit. By using them effectively, you can make your AI prompts more structured, clear, and easy to understand. Whether you're using triple backticks for code, triple quotes for long text, or XML-style tags for structured data, delimiters help the AI process your prompts more accurately and provide better responses.

** 🛠️ Building Awesome Step-by-Step Prompts

This step-by-step prompt is like giving your friend a detailed itinerary for their road trip. 📝 It guides your AI through the task, ensuring a well-organized and useful response.

** 🤔 Why Should You Care About Output Length?

Imagine you're packing for a trip. 🧳 If you don't specify how long you'll be gone, you might end up with too many clothes or not enough. Similarly, when you ask an AI to generate text without specifying the length, you might get a response that's either too long or too short. 😕

Specifying the desired output length is like telling your AI, "I'm going on a 3-day trip, so I need just enough clothes for that duration." 👕👖👗 This helps ensure that the AI's response is:

1. 🎯 Efficient: You get the information you need without wasting time on extra details.
2. 📎 Relevant: The AI focuses on providing information that directly answers your question.
3. 📋 Format-friendly: The response fits the specific format requirements you have in mind, like a report, article, or summary.

** 🛠️ Tools for Controlling Output Length

Just like you have different tools for packing your suitcase, like packing cubes or compression bags, there are various strategies you can use to control the length of AI-generated text. 🧰

*** 1️⃣ Setting Explicit Length Parameters

When possible, tell your AI exactly how long you want the response to be. This can be in the form of word count, sentence number, paragraph count, or bullet points. For instance:

=Please generate a summary of the article in three sentences.=

*** 2️⃣ Using Comparative Instructions

You can also guide the AI on the desired length by comparing it with well-known formats. For example:

=Please write a summary as concise as a tweet.=

*** 3️⃣ Leveraging Examples

Provide examples of the desired length. This approach gives the AI a clear reference to match:

=Like the following example, provide a brief overview of the topic: [Insert example]=

*** 4️⃣ Iterative Refinement

Start with a broad request and refine based on the initial output. This iterative approach can help you get the exact length you need:

=Please summarize the following text: [Insert text]   [AI generates summary]   Now, condense this summary into three key points.=

*** 5️⃣ Specifying Information Density

Requesting a specific number of facts or points within a given structure can indirectly control length:

=Please list the top five benefits of exercise.=

** 🌈 Putting It into Practice

Let's apply these strategies to a real-world scenario:

*Approach:*

1. 📏 Setting Explicit Length Parameters:

   =Summarize the product's features like this example: [Insert example summary]=

   =What is the new product about? [AI responds] Can you distill this to three key benefits?=

5. 🎯 Specifying Information Density:
   =Please list the top three unique selling points of the new product.=
