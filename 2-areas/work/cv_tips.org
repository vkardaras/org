 #+title: CV Tips

* Getting through the Applicant Tracking System (ATS)

1. Use Relevant Keywords
   - Incorporate *keywords* and *phrases* directly from the *job description* into your resume.
   - ATS systems scan for *specific terms related to job requirements*
2. Optimize Formatting
   - Stick to a clean and simple format with standard fonts like Arial, Calibri or Times New Roman
   - Use a logical structure with *clear headings, bullet points*, and *consistent spacing*
   - Avoid using graphics, images or elaborate designs
3. Include a *skills section*
   - Create a *dedicated skills section* listing relevant *hard* and *soft skills*
   - Use *industry-specific terminology* and include a *diverse range of skills* mentioned in the *job description*
4. Tailor your Resume
   - *Customize your resume* for each job application, emphasizing *experiences* and *achievements* that directly relate to the *job requirements*
5. Use standard headings
   - Label your sections with *standard headings* such as *WORK EXPERIENCE*, *EDUCATION* and *SKILLS*
   - This helps ATS systems *categorize* and *check your information accurately*
6. Avoid *unusual characters*
   - Stick to *standard characters* and formatting like bullets and dashes
   - Avoid *special characters*, *symbols*, or *fancy formatting* that might *confuse* the ATS
7. Include relevant details
   - Provide *specific details* about your *work experience*, *education*, and *achievements*
   - *Quantify your accomplishments* with numbers, percentages, and metrics wherever possible
   - This not only makes your resume *more impactful* for the *ATS* but also for *human reviewers*
8. Check *file compatibility*
   - Submit your resume in a *compatible file format*, such as .docs or pdf
   - Avoid using formats that might *not be compatible with the ATS*, like image files or unconventional document types
9. Proofread carefully
   - Make sure your resume is *free of spelling mistakes*, *grammatical errors*, and *formatting issues*
   - ATS systems can be *sensitive to errors*, and even minor mistakes could cause your resume to be *overlooked*
10. Test your Resume
    - Before submitting your resume, use ATS-friendly tools or software to test *how well it fares against common ATS algorithms*
    - Use tools like [[https://www.jobscan.co/][jobscan]]

* Creating an Engaging Resume

1. Tailor your *Resume* to *Each Job*
   - Tailoring your resume to each job isn't just a suggestion - *it's a must-do*
     Candidates who customize their resumes for specific job descriptions are 50% more likely to receive an interview invitation.
     54% of job applicants do not update their resumes to each position.
   - Always keep your resume *up-to-date* with any new *role change*, *qualification*, or any other *relevant information*.
2. Use *Action-Packed* Verbs
   - Let your *achievements* do the talking with *action-packed verbs that command attention*.
     Action verbs boost your chance of being offered an interview by 140%
     e.g. instead of "Managed a team..." use "Led a high-performing team to surpass quarterly targets."
3. Speak with *Confidence* in the *Active voice*
   - Your resume is your chance to *showcase your confidence and expertise*.
     Writing in the active voice not only exudes confidence but also makes your resume easier to read and digest
     e.g. Responsibilities included overseeing project timelines -> I oversaw project timelines to ensure timely completion and delivery.
4. Embrace *White Space* for *Readability*
   - Not only does it enhance readability, but it also draws the reader in and keeps them engaged.
     Recruiters will be reading many resumes and if your is *clearly laid out* with white space it gives the reader a *sense of ease in the sifting process*
5. Showcase your *Unique Skill Set*
   - *Your skills are what set you apart from the competition*, so don't be shy about showing them off.
     48% of recruiters use skills data to help fill their roles.
6. Address *Potential Concerns* head-on
   - Anticipate any potential *concerns* or *questions* that hiring managers may have and *address them head-on in your resume*.
     Whether it's explaining a career gap or showcasing remote working skills, proactive transparency can go a long way in building trust and credibility.
     *Whatever you do, do not lie!*
7. Mirror the *Company Culture*
   - Showcasing your *understanding of* and *alignment with the company culture* can *significantly boost* your chances of success
     84% of recruiters believe that culture fit is crucial when evaluating candidates.
     So, mirror the company's values and language in your resume to demonstrate your compatibility and commitment. You should have an understanding of what the company culture is from your research.
8. Polish your resume to *perfection*
   - A single typo could be the *difference* between *landing and interview* and *being passed over*
     Attention to detail speaks volumes about your professionalism and dedication.
     51% of documents have grammatical errors

 
* Making a master Resume or CV - Working through the different sections

** Master Resume/CV
This comprehensive document serves as a place to keep all your *skills*, *experiences*, and *accomplishments*, making it *easier to tailor specific resumes for different job applications*

** Guide on how to create an effective master resume

This document serves as a *source* from which you can extract the *most relevant details* to create a *targeted resume* for each job your apply for

** Benefits of having a master resume
1. Give a Comprehensive Overview
   - Professional Experiences:
     List all your *roles*, including *part-time jobs*, *internships*, and *volunteer work*
   - Education
     Include all *degrees*, *certifications*, *workshops*, and *training programs*
   - Skills
     Catalog all your *technical* and *soft skills*, including *languages*, *software proficiency*, project management*, and *leadership abilities*
2. Put everything in *chronological order*
   - Arrange your experiences in *reverse chronological order*
     This helps recruiters see your *most recent* and *relevant experiences first*
3. Detailed Descriptions
   - Provide detailed *descriptions* for each *role*, highlighting your *responsibilities*, *accomplishments*, and *contributions*
   - Use *action verbs* and *quantify your achievements* whenever possible
     e.g. Instead of *Manages a team* use *Led a team of 10, improving project completion times by 20%*
4. Create a skills inventory
   - Create a *dedicated section* for listing your *skills*. This should include both *technical skills* and *soft skills*
5. Put together all education and training
   - List all your *educational qualifications* and *professional development* initiatives
   - Include the *institution's name*, *degree* or *certification earned*, and *graduation date*
6. Include any *professional developments*
   - Include any professional development activities such as *workshops*, *seminars*, *conferences*, or *online courses*
   - You can also include a section with *hyperlinks* for your *portfolio* if this is something *relevant* to you

This document not only helps you *stay organized* but also ensures that *you are always ready to present your most relevant* and *impressive qualifications* to potential employers

** Master Resume/CV sections

*** Professional Summary

- A professional summary is a *concise*, *impactful statement that highlights your career goals*, *key skills*, and *what you bring to the table.
- Think of it as a "hook" to get the *attention of the reader* and make them want to *read the rest of your resume*

**** Tips for a professional summary

1. Conciseness
   - Keep your summary brief
     This ensures that hiring managers *quickly grasp your strengths* and *qualification* without wading through unnecessary details
   - Example: 
   #+begin_example
   I am an experienced financial anlyst with a strong background in investment strategies and portfolio management. Adept at risk assessment and frinancial forecasting to optimize investment returns.
   #+end_example

2. Writing to the *job roles
   
   - Always *tailor* your resume to the *job description*
   - Align your summary with the *specific job role* you're applying for
   - Highlight relevant *skills* and *experiences* to show that *you're not just a fit, but a perfect fit for the position*
   - Example: 
   #+begin_example
   I am a dynamic marketing manager with over 7 years of experience in digital marketing, SEO, and content strategy. Proven track record of increasing online engegement and driving brand growth.
   #+end_example
     
3. Introduction
   
   - Begin with a *strong opening statement* that sums up your *overall career objective* or *professional expertise*
   - Example:
   #+begin_example
   I am a seasoned marketing proffessional with over a decade of experience in digital strategy and brand managemenht
   #+end_example

4. Skills and Expertise
   
   - Highlight your *core skills* and *areas of expertise*
   - Use *action verbs* to convey your *capabilities* effectively
   - Example: 
   #+begin_example
   Proficient in leveraging data analytics to drive strategic marketing decisions
   #+end_example

5. Experience

   - Provide a *glimpse of your relevant work experience*, mentioning *key accomplishments* or *roles*
   - Example: 
   #+begin_example
   I successfully led a team of 15 in the development of a high-impact social media campaign than increased brand engagement by 45% at XYZ Corporation.
   #+end_example

6. Adaptability

   - Show your *ability to adapt to different environments* or *industries*
   - This demonstrates *versatility* and a *willingness to learn*
   - Example: 
   #+begin_example
   By combining my creative vision with a data-driven approach, I excel at transforming insights into compelling marketing campaigns.
   #+end_example

7. Professional Tone

   - Maintain a *professional tone* throughout the summary
   - Avoid *informal language* or *unnecessary jargon* to ensure *clarity* and professionalism*
   - Write in *first person*
   - Example: 
   #+begin_example
   I am a dedicated marketing professional with experience supporting digital and offline campaings, conducting market research, and managing social media. I thrive in fast-paced environments and am passionate about driving brand awareness and engagement. I look forward to leveraging my skills and enthusiasm to contribute effvectively to your team.
   #+end_example

8. Proofreading

   - Carefully *proofread* the summary for any *grammatical errors* or *typos*
   - *Clarity* and *professionalism* in your summary are key. These are the *first things* the recruiter will *read*
   - 59% of recruiters will *reject a candidate* because of *poor grammar* or a *spelling error*

*** Key Achievements

1. Use *bullet points* to list your achievements
   - Example: 
     Achievements
     * Increased Efficiency: Improved perational efficiency by 35% by implementing Lean Six Sigma methodologies at XYZ Corp.
     * Market Expansion: Expanded market reach by 20% through targeted international marketing campaigns, resulting in $1.5 million in additional revenue.
     * Cost Reduction: Achieved a 15% reduction in annual operating costs by streamlining supply chain processes at ABC Logistics.
     * Innovation Award: Won the 2022 Innovation Award at DEF Tech for developing a new software solution that reduced customer service response times by 50%.
     * High-impact Training: Designed and conducted a training program that increased team productivity by 25% and was adopted company-wide at GHI Enterprises.
   - These examples demonstrate not only *soft skills* but also *result delivered*
   - This makes the results *tangible* and *relatable*
2. Make your achievements *easy for the recruiter to review*
   - Keeping it concise: Use brief, impactful *bullet points*
   - Highlighting Key Metrics: Use *numerical results* for quick visibility and further emphasis on your achievements
   - Using Strong Action Verbs: Start each bullet point with a *strong action verb*
   - Being Specific: Avoid *vague statements*. Specific details make your achievements *more compelling*
3. *Tailor this section* for different job applications
   Customize this section in important because *it shows the imployer what value you can add to their organization*
   - Analyze the job description: Identify the *key skills* and *experiences* the employer is looking for.
   - Select Relevant Achievements: Choose achievements that *best match the requirements* and *responsibilities* outlined in the job description.
   - Use Job-specific Keyword: *Integrate specific keywords from the job posting into your achievements* to align with the employer's needs. This will also *help you pass the ATS*
